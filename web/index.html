How it works
  1. Read the twist. Type the judge's twist in plain words. A parser (or the AI, when set up) turns it into settings; you can edit every one.
  2. Know the customer. Income, spending, fixed costs, household bills and savings, exactly as defined in the challenge, plus the customer's type.
  3. The shock. The official runway: how many months savings last after the shock.
  4. The plan. The cheapest set of cuts that stretches savings furthest, and an emergency-fund goal.
  5. Everyone. The same twist on all 4,500 customers. Customers who fall short sink on the globe.

Runway

Savings left after any one-off cost ÷ monthly shortfall (spending minus income), capped at 120 months. If income still covers spending, there is no shortfall.

Customer types (first match wins)

  • Stabilize first: balance went below zero in any month.
  • Pension-protected: pension is 30%+ of income, which a layoff doesn't cut.
  • Savings-first: 55% of income is above the 2026 EI maximum ($3,159/month).
  • Trim: everyone else.

The plan

Cuts only cash withdrawals, household bills and other transfers out, trying every combination in 5% steps, with limits by type. Every number comes from code; the language model never calculates.

Findings

An overdraft before the loan predicts default

All 27 borrowers who went below zero before taking out their loan defaulted. Among the other 655 borrowers, 7.5% did. None of the 606 borrowers who repaid ever went below zero.

Looking safe today is not being resilient

74% of customers spend less than they earn, yet 699 of them would run out of savings within 6 months of a layoff on EI.

The official EI rule flatters higher earners

Real EI stops at $3,159/month. With the cap, working customers who can't last 6 months rise from 42% to 61%.

What the plan fixes, and who it can't save

After a layoff on EI, the share who can't last 6 months falls from 35.1% to 6.5% with the plan. Even with the strongest cuts, 1 in 5 Stabilize-first customers still fall short.

76% of all spending is cash withdrawals, so plans say "lower cash withdrawals by $X" rather than guessing categories. The overdraft finding rests on 27 borrowers: a strong signal in this data, not a proven rule.
